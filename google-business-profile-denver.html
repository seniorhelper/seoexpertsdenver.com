Google Business Profile setup checklist for Denver businesses

For most local businesses, the Google Business Profile is the listing people see first: in Google Maps, in the local pack above the regular results, and increasingly in AI-generated answers. Here is how to set it up correctly and keep it working.

Quick Answer: What makes a strong Google Business Profile?

A verified profile with your real business name, the most specific primary category, accurate phone, hours and website, a correct address or service area, a complete list of services, recent real photos, and a steady flow of genuine reviews that you reply to. Accuracy and consistency matter more than any trick.

Why the Profile Matters So Much

When someone searches for a service plus “near me” or plus a neighborhood like Capitol Hill, Highlands or Cherry Creek, Google usually shows a map with a short list of businesses before any regular website results. Those listings come from Google Business Profiles. Many people call, ask for directions or visit a website straight from that list without ever scrolling further.

The same profile data feeds Google Maps and Google’s own AI features for local questions. A website can be excellent, but if the profile is incomplete, unverified or inconsistent, the business is easy to miss.

Step 1: Claim and Verify

Go to business.google.com and sign in with a Google account the business will control long term, ideally not an employee’s personal account. Search for your business name. If a listing already exists, request ownership; if not, create one.

Google then asks you to verify. Depending on the business, the options can include a short video recording of your location or equipment, a phone call, a text, an email or a mailed postcard. Until verification is complete, changes may not show and the listing may not appear reliably in Maps.

Step 2: Get the Core Details Right

  • Business name: use your real-world name exactly as it appears on your signage and paperwork. Adding keywords or city names to the name breaks Google’s guidelines and is a common cause of suspensions.
  • Primary category: choose the most specific category that describes your main service (for example, “Roofing contractor” rather than “Contractor”). Add secondary categories only for services you actually provide.
  • Phone and website: use a local or main line that is answered, and link to the most relevant page of your site.
  • Hours: keep regular hours current and add special hours before holidays so customers are not turned away.

Step 3: Address or Service Area

If customers come to you, show your address. If you travel to customers, Google’s guidelines say to hide the address and set a service area instead. Google lets you list up to 20 service areas and recommends keeping them within roughly a two-hour drive of where you are based. A Denver contractor might list Denver, Aurora, Lakewood, Englewood, Littleton and Arvada rather than the entire state.

Co-working spaces, virtual offices and P.O. boxes are generally not eligible as a business address. Using one is a frequent reason profiles get suspended.

Step 4: Fill In Services, Photos and Description

Add each service you offer with a short, plain description. Write a business description that explains what you do and who you serve in normal sentences, not a list of keywords. Upload real photos of your work, your team, your vehicles or your storefront, and keep adding a few new ones over time. Stock photos add little.

Step 5: Build a Healthy Review Habit

Reviews influence both where you appear and whether people choose you. Ask every satisfied customer, using the review link available in your profile, soon after the job is done. Google’s policies prohibit offering discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, asking only happy customers, or posting reviews on a customer’s behalf, so keep the process simple and honest.

Reply to every review. A short, specific thank-you works for positive reviews. For negative ones, stay calm, acknowledge the issue and offer to continue the conversation offline. Future customers read the replies as closely as the reviews.

Step 6: Keep It Accurate

Profiles drift. Anyone can suggest edits to a public listing, and Google sometimes applies them. Sign in monthly, check for suggested changes, confirm your details, and look at the Performance section to see how many calls, website visits and direction requests the profile is producing. Make sure your name, address and phone number match exactly on your website, Apple Maps, Bing, Yelp, Facebook and industry directories.

Tip: Add UTM parameters to the website link in your profile (for example, ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp) so Google Analytics can show how many visits and leads come from the listing itself.

Questions about this topic

Does a Google Business Profile cost anything?

No. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. Google Ads can appear on Maps and in local results, but those are a separate, paid product. You never need to pay to create, verify or keep a profile.

Can I have a Google Business Profile without a storefront?

Yes. Businesses that serve customers at the customer’s location, such as plumbers, cleaners and mobile services, can create a service-area profile. Google’s guidelines ask you to hide your street address in that case and list the cities or ZIP codes you serve instead.

How often should I update my Google Business Profile?

Check it at least once a month. Confirm hours, phone number and services are still correct, add a few recent photos, reply to new reviews, and set special hours before holidays. Also review any suggested edits Google shows you, because anyone can suggest changes to a public listing.

Why did my business disappear from Google Maps?

Common causes include an unverified or suspended profile, a recent change to the business name, address or category that triggered a re-review, or a listing that breaks Google’s guidelines (for example, extra keywords added to the business name). Sign in to your profile to see its status, fix anything that does not match your real-world business, and request reinstatement if it was suspended.
