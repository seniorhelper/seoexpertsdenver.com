Your Google Business Profile was suspended. Here is the way back.

For a local business, a suspended profile can mean the Maps listing, the reviews and the calls disappear in a day. The appeal process is real, but it rewards patience and preparation. An appeal filed in a hurry, before the cause is fixed, is the most common way to make a suspension last longer.

Quick answer: how do I get a suspended Google Business Profile reinstated?

Bring the profile into line with Google's guidelines for representing your business first, then submit one appeal through Google's appeal tool with evidence such as a business license, registration, tax certificate or utility bill showing the same name and address as the profile. Google says decisions can take up to five business days, an appeal can be submitted only once, and a denied appeal can get one further review only with new evidence. Do not create a new profile for the same business.

Suspended, disabled or restricted: read the notice carefully

Google now uses more than one label. A profile can be suspended or disabled for not following the guidelines, specific content can be restricted, and sometimes the restriction sits on the Google account rather than the profile. If the account is restricted, Google's help pages say you must appeal on your account first, then the profile once the account restriction is lifted. Read the email and the Business Profile notice exactly as written before doing anything.

Some owners also describe a "soft" suspension, where the profile still shows publicly but cannot be managed, and a "hard" one, where it disappears from Search and Maps. Those are community terms rather than Google's own, but they are a useful way to judge urgency.

What usually triggers a suspension

Google does not always say why. These are the patterns that come up most often, and each one maps to a specific part of Google's guidelines.

Keywords added to the business name

"Smith Plumbing" becoming "Smith Plumbing Denver Emergency Plumber". The name should match your real-world name on signage and documents.

An ineligible address

Virtual offices, coworking addresses without staffed hours, PO boxes or mailbox stores. A service-area business working from home should hide the address.

Duplicate or extra profiles

A second profile for the same business at the same address, or separate profiles for each service you offer.

Big edits all at once

Changing name, address, category and phone in one sitting can trigger a review, especially on a newer profile.

Category or industry issues

Some categories, such as locksmiths and garage door repair, face extra scrutiny for fraud and may need advanced verification.

Ownership or manager changes

A former employee or agency with management access making changes, or a dispute over who owns the profile.

Fix the cause before you appeal

Because each appeal can be submitted only once, the order matters. Before filing, we go through the profile line by line against Google's guidelines and correct anything that does not match reality: business name exactly as on your signage and registration, address shown or hidden correctly, a primary category that describes the business rather than a keyword you want to rank for, hours you actually keep, and a website that names the same business at the same location.

We also check outside the profile. If your website, your Colorado business registration and your listings on other sites show different names or addresses, reviewers notice. Consistency is part of the evidence.

Evidence that strengthens an appeal

Google's help center lists the kinds of documents that can help, and notes they should show a name and address that match the profile. For a Colorado business, the useful set usually includes:

  • A business license or registration, such as the record from the Colorado Secretary of State's business database.
  • A tax certificate or other official registration document.
  • A recent utility bill in the business's name at that address.
  • For storefronts, clear photos of permanent exterior signage and the entrance, taken so the location can be identified.
  • For trades that need one, the relevant state or city license showing the same business name.

Community experts on Google's own forum add a few practical points: keep the explanation short and factual, say what the business does and whether the address should be shown or hidden, and avoid emotional or accusatory language. Reviewers are deciding a factual question.

How the appeal process works

StageWhat happens
SubmitThrough Google's Business Profile appeals tool, with evidence attached. One appeal per decision.
ReviewGoogle says reviews and decisions can take up to five business days. Do not edit the profile or create a new one while you wait.
ApprovedThe profile is reinstated. Check that categories, hours and reviews look right, and keep future edits gradual.
DeniedYou can request an additional review, but only with new evidence that was not in the original appeal.

Do not create a replacement profile. Google's help pages say not to create a new profile for the same business. It does not solve the problem, and it can tie the new profile to the same issue.

While you wait

A suspension cuts off one channel, not all of them. Make sure your website ranks for your own business name, that your phone number is easy to find on it, and that your listings elsewhere are accurate. If the suspension drags on and the business depends on local calls, short-term paid search can bridge the gap. Once the profile is back, we help you keep it healthy, with edits made slowly and documented.

Questions people ask

How long does a Google Business Profile appeal take?

Google's help center says appeal reviews and decisions can take up to five business days. Complex cases can take longer, particularly when an account-level restriction has to be resolved first.

Should I create a new profile if mine is suspended?

No. Google advises against creating a new profile for the same business. Fix the existing profile and appeal.

Can I appeal more than once?

Each appeal can be submitted only once. If it is denied, you can ask for an additional review, but only with new evidence that was not included the first time. That is why preparation matters.

Will I lose my reviews if my profile is suspended?

While a profile is suspended, its reviews may not show publicly. In most reinstatements the existing reviews come back with the profile, which is another reason not to start a new one.

Can a home-based business have a Google Business Profile?

Yes, if it serves customers in person, for example at their homes. A service-area business run from home should hide its address and set service areas instead. Listing a home address as a storefront when customers do not visit is a common cause of trouble.
