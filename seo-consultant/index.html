Quick answer: what does an SEO consultant do?

An SEO consultant advises rather than produces. They review your data and your plans, decide what should be done and in what order, check work done by your staff, developers or agency, and coach your team. You keep control of the doing, and you pay for experienced judgment instead of hours of production you may already have in-house.

Who SEO consulting suits

Consulting works best when the business already has hands but lacks direction. Typical situations we see in Denver:

A marketing coordinator who inherited SEO

Capable, busy, and handed "the website" without training. Needs a plan, priorities, and someone to check the hard calls.

A business with a web developer but no SEO lead

The developer builds what is asked. Someone needs to decide what should be asked, and review changes before they ship.

An owner who wants to understand the work

Some owners prefer to learn enough to manage SEO well themselves, rather than handing it off entirely.

A company already paying an agency

Wants an independent view on whether the strategy, the reports and the spend make sense.

Coaching an in-house team

Good coaching is not a slideshow on ranking factors. It is your team working on your site, with an expert explaining why. Over the course of a program, staff learn to:

  • Read Search Console properly: what the Performance and Page indexing reports really say, and when a change is noise.
  • Research what customers search for and decide which page should answer each question.
  • Write and update service pages that are useful and specific, not padded.
  • Maintain the Google Business Profile without tripping the guidelines.
  • Spot the warning signs of a technical problem before it costs traffic.
  • Brief developers and writers clearly, and review their work.

Zach Wennstedt learned SEO the hard way, as a business owner who could not afford for it to fail. Coaching passes on that practical, conversion-first view: the goal is calls and customers, not a prettier report.

Second opinions on agency work

If you already pay an agency, an independent review can be the best money you spend this year, whichever way it comes out. We look at what was promised, what was delivered, and whether the reporting connects to leads. Sometimes the answer is that the agency is doing good work and needs more time. Sometimes the work does not match the invoice. Either way, you get a clear written view and the questions to raise.

We do not use second opinions as a sales tactic. If your current provider is doing the right things, we will tell you to stay.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between an SEO consultant and an SEO agency?

A consultant mainly advises and reviews, while an agency mainly produces: content, links, technical changes and campaigns, usually with a team. Many businesses use a consultant to set direction and either their own staff or an agency to carry it out.

How much does an SEO consultant charge?

Rates vary widely. In Ahrefs' published poll of 439 SEO providers, the most common hourly band was $75 to $100, with many experienced consultants charging more. We quote each consulting format as a fixed price before it starts.

Can you train my marketing person to do SEO?

Yes. The coaching program is designed for exactly that, using your own website and data as the training material so the skills apply immediately.

Will you review our current agency's work?

Yes. An independent review looks at strategy, deliverables, reporting and results, and ends with a written assessment. If the agency is doing good work, the review will say so.

Do consulting sessions have to be in person?

No. Most sessions happen by video or phone, which suits businesses anywhere in Colorado.
