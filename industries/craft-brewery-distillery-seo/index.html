SEO for Colorado breweries, distilleries and wineries

Colorado's craft beverage scene is famous and crowded, and it is getting tougher. When fewer people are drinking and more taprooms are closing, being the place that shows up when someone nearby searches for a brewery tonight matters more than ever.

Quick answer: how should a Colorado brewery approach SEO?

Start with Google Maps: an accurate, guideline-compliant Business Profile with current hours, real photos and steady reviews drives most taproom visits from search. Then make the website answer what visitors check before they go: what is pouring, food, events, dog and kid policies, parking, and hours. Keep those pages current, because outdated information costs visits and trust.

A tougher market than the reputation suggests

Colorado is still one of the country's best-known craft beer states, but the boom has turned. Industry coverage of Colorado Brewers Guild figures reported that 41 Colorado breweries closed in 2024, after 35 in 2023, and state liquor data showed beer sales declining faster than the national craft trend. Distilleries and wineries face similar pressure on discretionary spending.

In a shrinking market, the taprooms that keep their regulars and win new visitors are often the easiest to find and the clearest about what they offer. That is a search problem as much as a beer problem.

Google Maps is the main event

For a taproom, the Business Profile often matters more than the website. The details that make the biggest difference are basic and frequently wrong:

  • Hours, including special hours. Holiday hours, private events and seasonal patio changes. Nothing loses a visit faster than showing "open" to someone at a locked door.
  • Primary category. Brewery, brewpub, distillery, winery or bar each signal different things.
  • Attributes. Outdoor seating, dog-friendly, family-friendly and accessibility details, where Google offers them.
  • Photos you took. The bar, the patio, the food, the space, kept current as things change.
  • Reviews and replies. Ask happy visitors without incentives, and reply to criticism calmly. Google's policies prohibit offering rewards for reviews.

Name rules trip up breweries more than most. Adding "Craft Beer, Food Trucks, Live Music" to the business name on Google is against the guidelines and a common suspension trigger; see what to do if a profile is suspended.

Website fixes that matter most

Most brewery websites are built to look good, which they should, but they often bury what visitors need.

  • The tap list as text. An image of a chalkboard cannot be searched or read aloud. A simple text list, updated when it changes, serves people and search engines.
  • Events as real pages. Each recurring event described in text with day, time and details, instead of a social media embed only.
  • The visit page. Address, hours, parking, transit, accessibility, dogs, kids, food: everything someone checks before leaving home.
  • Private events and to-go sales. These often carry the best margins and deserve their own clear pages.
  • Age gates done carefully. A pop-up age check is common for alcohol brands; make sure it does not block search engines from reading the page content.

Advertising rules worth respecting

Alcohol marketing is regulated at federal and state level, and responsible-marketing codes from industry bodies add further expectations. SEO content should follow the same care as any other marketing: no claims aimed at people under 21, no health claims, and accurate information about what you sell and where. If you are unsure about a specific claim, check with your licensing counsel before publishing.

Questions people ask

What matters most for a brewery's Google ranking?

For nearby searches, the Business Profile: accurate category and hours, real photos, steady reviews and the distance from the searcher. The website supports it by confirming the details and answering visitors' questions.

Can I add "live music" or "craft beer" to my business name on Google?

No. Google's guidelines say the name should match your real-world name. Adding descriptive keywords is a common reason profiles get suspended.

Should our tap list be on our website?

Yes, as text rather than an image, and kept current. It helps visitors decide to come and gives search engines something specific to understand.

Can we offer free beer for reviews?

No. Google's policies prohibit offering incentives in exchange for reviews. Ask satisfied visitors to share honest feedback instead.
