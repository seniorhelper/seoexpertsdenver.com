Quick answer: how do I get my business into Google's AI Overviews?

According to Google, a page is eligible to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode if it is indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet. There are no extra technical requirements, no special schema and no AI text file to add. Inclusion then depends on whether Google's systems judge your page a helpful, relevant source for that specific question, which comes down to the same fundamentals as ranking: clear, accurate, original content on a site Google can crawl.

What Google has actually said

A lot of AI search advice is speculation. Google's own documentation is shorter and more useful. It is the baseline we work from for Google's features; other assistants such as ChatGPT and Perplexity use their own retrieval and are discussed separately in our GEO guide.

  • Eligibility is ordinary. Indexed and snippet-eligible pages can be shown as supporting links. Nothing additional is required.
  • No special files or markup. Google says there is no need for new machine-readable files, AI text files or special schema.org markup, and its optimization guide says Google Search ignores llms.txt files.
  • Controls work the same way. nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex limit how content can appear in AI features as well as in regular results.
  • Not every search shows one. AI Overviews appear only when Google's systems judge they add value, so many queries never trigger them.
  • The traffic is counted. Clicks from AI features are included in the Search Console Performance report under the Web search type, and Google has begun rolling out dedicated generative AI performance reports to some sites.

The practical takeaway: if someone is selling an "AI Overviews package" built on special markup or an llms.txt file, ask them to show you where Google says that helps. For Google's features, optimizing for AI search is still SEO.

Why a competitor gets cited and you do not

When we look at specific queries for Denver businesses, the reasons tend to fall into a few groups.

Your page is not eligible

Blocked from indexing, a canonical pointing elsewhere, a nosnippet tag added by a plugin, or content only visible after scripts run. Easy to miss and quick to fix.

The answer is buried

The fact the AI needs, such as a price range, a timeline or a requirement, is hidden mid-paragraph on a page about something broader, or only in an image.

Their page answers the actual question

Overviews often respond to longer, more specific questions. A competitor with a page on exactly that question has the edge over a general service page.

Thin evidence of expertise

Pages with no author, no specifics and no sources give little reason to be chosen over pages that show first-hand knowledge.

Inconsistent facts about you

Different names, addresses or service descriptions across your site and other listings make your business harder to identify with confidence.

You do rank, but the query does not show you

The Overview may cite only a few sources, and they can vary from one search to the next. Being absent from one check is not proof of a problem.

Measuring the impact on your traffic

The most common complaint is not "we are not cited" but "our clicks fell while our rankings did not". That pattern, steady impressions and position with falling click-through, is worth confirming before acting. For context, SparkToro's 2024 study of Datos panel data estimated 58.5% of US Google searches ended without a click to any website, and that measured period largely predates the wide rollout of AI Overviews.

We pull the affected queries from Search Console, check which of them now show an AI Overview or other feature above the results, and separate informational queries, where fewer clicks are expected, from the commercial ones that bring leads. Losing clicks on "what is a lien waiver" matters far less than losing them on "construction lawyer Denver".

Questions people ask

Do I need special schema markup to appear in AI Overviews?

Google says no special schema.org structured data is required for its AI features. Structured data can still help in other ways when it accurately describes visible content, but it is not a ticket into AI Overviews.

Should I add an llms.txt file?

For Google, it will not help. Google's AI optimization guide says Google Search ignores llms.txt files. Some other AI tools may read them, but there is little published evidence they affect citations.

Can I opt out of AI Overviews?

You can limit how your content appears using nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet or noindex, but these apply to regular search snippets too. Restricting AI use this way usually means accepting less visibility in Search as well.

How do I see AI Overview traffic in Search Console?

Google counts clicks from AI features in the Performance report under the Web search type. Google has also started rolling out dedicated generative AI performance reports to a subset of sites.

Can you guarantee my business will be cited?

No. Citations vary by query and can change from one search to the next. We can fix eligibility problems and make your pages the clearest answer available, which improves the odds.
